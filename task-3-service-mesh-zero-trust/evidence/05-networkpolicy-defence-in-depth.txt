----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE NETWORK LAYER CATCHES THAT THE MESH DOES NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Istio authorization is enforced BY THE SIDECAR, inside the pod.
  Anything that avoids the sidecar avoids the policy:
    - a pod created without the injection label
    - a workload that removes or bypasses its own proxy after
      compromise (the proxy is in its blast radius; the CNI is not)
    - ports Istio was never told to capture
  NetworkPolicy is enforced by the CNI, outside the pod, so it still
  applies in every one of those cases.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE MESH CATCHES THAT THE NETWORK LAYER CANNOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Identity. reporting and unauthorised-client are the same namespace,
  the same node and the same pod CIDR - see the addresses in
  03-authorization-identity.txt. No NetworkPolicy can admit one and
  refuse the other, because at L3/L4 there is nothing to tell them
  apart. Nor can it express 'GET /transactions but not POST'.

  Pod IPs are also recycled. An address-based allow-list silently
  transfers authority to whatever workload lands on that IP next.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
ORDER OF ENFORCEMENT, AND A USEFUL DIAGNOSTIC
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  The CNI drops packets before Envoy sees them, so NetworkPolicy is the
  outer gate. A request refused there produces a timeout with NO entry
  in the sidecar access log. So: a denial that is invisible in the
  Istio logs was dropped below the mesh - which tells you immediately
  which layer to debug.
